Pairs Trading Lab

Pairs Trading Lab is a free online tool that simulates how statistical pairs trading works: it generates a synthetic cointegrated stock pair in your browser, estimates the hedge ratio with OLS regression, tracks the spread's z-score against ±σ entry/exit bands, and backtests the mean-reversion trade with a full trade log.

No live market data, no sign-up, no API keys — every price series is simulated with a seedable random generator, so the experiment is reproducible. TICKER A / TICKER B are fictional.

How to read this

Practical pairs trading is a statistical bet: two prices share a long-run equilibrium, so when they drift apart, the spread should snap back. This Lab walks through the whole pipeline on simulated data, in five steps:

  1. Generate. Stock A is a random walk; the spread follows a mean-reverting process (Ornstein–Uhlenbeck) with speed θ, plus optional smooth drift and a one-time regime shift halfway through; Stock B = β·A + spread.
  2. Estimate β — without peeking. Regress B on A over a 60-day formation window only (the shaded region on the spread chart). The residual is the estimated spread. Fitting on the full sample would be cheating: demeaning a random walk manufactures fake mean reversion, making even a broken pair look tradable.
  3. Score it. A rolling 60-day z-score normalises the spread, so "+2" means the same thing on day 100 and day 200.
  4. Test stationarity. A simplified ADF check asks whether the spread genuinely mean-reverts. t-stat below −2.86 ≈ stationary.
  5. Trade the bands. Short the spread above +entry σ (short B, long β̂·A), buy it below −entry σ, exit near zero. Positions are sized to $10,000 notional per leg, the way a real pairs book would size them — a flat per-side cost is then economically meaningful.

The core formulas

β̂ = Cov(A, B) / Var(A)
zt = (spreadt − μ60) / σ60
half-life = ln(2) / |γ|  ·  from Δspreadt = γ·spreadt−1 + ε

What the presets teach

  • Tight pair — fast mean reversion, small noise: bands are thin, β̂ is accurate, the strategy harvests small frequent wins.
  • Sloppy pair — slow reversion, big noise: fewer trades, wider swings, costs bite harder.
  • Drifting pair — barely any pull-back: the "stationary" verdict gets shaky and half-life explodes.
  • Broken pair — a mid-sample regime shift: the spread jumps to a new level and never comes home. Fading the jump is the classic pairs-trade blow-up — the heuristic flags it "not stationary" and the trade log shows the damage.

Honest limitations

  • The stationarity check is a simplified heuristic, not a formal ADF test (no lag selection, no MacKinnon p-values).
  • The backtest assumes you can always trade at the simulated close, with one flat cost per side — real fills have slippage, borrow fees, and corporate actions. Positions are sized to $10,000 notional per leg, the way a real pairs book would size them.
  • β is estimated once on the formation window and frozen — in live trading it drifts and must be re-estimated, which is itself a source of blow-ups.
  • Everything here is synthetic. This page teaches the mechanics; it is not investment advice and is not a recommendation to trade any security.
